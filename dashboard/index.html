How the radar scores

implied value = TVL × category multiple × growth multiplier × maturity multiplier × chain breadth

The four multipliers

  • Category multiple — what a launch is worth per dollar of TVL, by what the protocol does: DEXs 0.35; lending, derivatives and prediction markets 0.30; yield, yield aggregators, leveraged farming and basis trading 0.28; everything else 0.25; RWA and restaking 0.22; CDP, chains and staking pools 0.20.
  • Growth multiplier — 1 + clamp(7-day change, −60%, +120%) × 0.5. A protocol up 10% in a week scores 5% higher than a flat one.
  • Maturity multiplier — 0.6 + min(age, 3 years) / 3 years × 0.4. Three years with no token is a full point; a month-old protocol is 0.63. An unknown listing date counts as one year.
  • Chain breadth — 1 + min(chains − 1, 4) × 0.03. Each extra chain up to four adds 3% for wider distribution at launch.

What counts as pre-token

The same rule the house watchlist uses, applied to every row: the snapshot records no token (no market cap, no Gecko id, no symbol), and the protocol is not an exchange, a bridge, a liquid-staking product, or a wrapped or staked product of one that trades, and its name is not a versioned copy. That leaves 197 of the 342 pre-token records in the snapshot: the 40 on the watchlist, 19 more above the $50M floor the house list does not name, and 138 below it.

What the board leaves out

145 pre-token records are not launches at all: exchanges with no token of their own (Bybit $13.8B, Bitfinex $12.6B, Robinhood $12.2B), wrapped assets (WBTC $7.9B), staked products (Binance staked ETH $7.4B) and bridges (Coinbase Bridge $7.2B). Ranking them would crown products that can never launch.

A reality check from the live side

The 24 largest live-token protocols in this snapshot trade at a median of about $1.19 of market cap per $1 of TVL. The model applies 0.20–0.35 — a launch discount against live comps, because a new token has no trading history, no liquidity depth and no proven fee capture.
